How this is calculated

Most pricing advice stops at the tray and the glue. That undercounts badly, in two places.

Waste is a real cost. Fans you discard, adhesive that cures on the jade stone, trays that dry out before they're finished. A 10–15% allowance is normal and pretending it's zero means every set quietly loses you money.

Overhead has to land somewhere. Booth rent doesn't care how many sets you do. Dividing monthly overhead by realistic monthly sets — not your best month — gives you the amount each set has to carry. This is the number that decides whether a full book is profitable or just busy.

The target price then works backwards from margin rather than marking up cost: price = true cost ÷ (1 − margin). A 45% margin means the price must be about 1.8× your cost, not 1.45×. Marking up by the margin percentage is the single most common pricing error in service businesses, and it leaves you short every time.

Your rate for chair time is deliberately not an input here. It isn't a cost — it's what's left after costs, and that's exactly what the hourly figure below tells you.
